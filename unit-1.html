Blog: Consequences of Teens Using Phones

When coming up with my phenomenon, I was trying to observe IU’s campus to write into my journal for English. As I looked at the kids around me, I noticed a very concerning trend: everyone was always on their phones. I began to watch closer, and I ended up seeing that these very teens that I go to school with are so busy with their phones, they block out the whole world around them. I then thought about what they could be doing, like texting friends, changing their music, or even just scrolling on social media.

As I wrote down more and more into my journal, it brought up a question about what negative effects could this phone problem be causing on these kids? This is where I began my research in finding out the negative effects that phones bring onto teens, whether it be mentally or physically. I researched it, and using information from the National Institute of Health (NIH) and The Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), I found out that phones are having a negative effect on teens, and that my phenomenon I found most definitely real. These sources talked a lot about how it causes social issues, anxiety, stress, and even posterior muscle problems.

A few other questions that are still left unanswered so far in my researching are:

Does this have life-lasting effects?

Should teens in college specifically cut back on phones while in public?

Is this phone usage impacting actual learning?

The three popular culture artifacts that I selected are Snapchat, AirPods/Headphones, and Tik Tok. When looking around campus most of the time I see people with some type of headphones on. These devices could expand on my current interpretation because they are sometimes a major reason/addition to the problem of cellphones. Sometimes, these devices can distract somebody from what is going on around them because they can’t hear anything, which completely disorients them. This can cause them not to hear a car, bike, or other people around them. The use of headphones is a huge reason for kids not paying attention, and being on their phones.

While the usage of phones in college students could be for activities like communicating with parents, studying for tests, or checking for important emails, many students use them for social media and other distracting apps that limit their awareness to what is around them, and what is actually going on.
